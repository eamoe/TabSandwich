### New

- **A welcome page** after a fresh install: pin the icon, save your first page, and the keyboard shortcut.
- **Reminders.** While editing a tab, choose **Remind me tomorrow** or **next week**. At 9:00 that day you get a notification, and the tab shows under Waiting until you open it. Chrome asks about notifications once, with your first reminder; say no and the tab still shows under Waiting.
- **A waiting count on the toolbar icon**, if you'd like one. Turn it on in Settings › General: it shows how many tabs are waiting, reminders due included.
- **Address-bar search.** Type `ts`, a space and a word in Chrome's address bar to jump straight to a saved tab. Nothing found? Enter opens the library on that search.
- **A full-page library** for big collections: categories and saved windows in a sidebar, a wide table that sorts by any column, bulk editing and cleanup tips, quick even with thousands of tabs. Open it from the new **Open in** menu in the popup's header, or right-click the toolbar icon and choose **Open library**.
- **A side panel** that stays open beside the page while you browse. Open it from the **Open in** menu in the popup, or make it what the toolbar icon opens in Settings › General. The first Save from the panel asks Chrome once to let it see the page you're on.
- **Right-click to save.** Right-click any link or page, then **Save to Tab Sandwich** and a category. The link's own words become its title, it shows up in anything you have open, and the toolbar icon flashes ✓. A link you'd already saved moves to the category you picked.

### Changed

- **Titles stop at 300 characters**, far longer than any real page title. In "Add link" and when editing a tab, the title field stops taking text there, like a note at 120. A page, a whole window or a backup with a longer title is shortened to 300 characters, ending in "…". Titles you've already saved stay as they are until you edit them.

### Under the hood

- A small background worker joins the popup, which is what makes the rest of this release possible. Everything open stays up to date by itself, and saves from different places can never overwrite each other.
- From this release on, each release is published with its notes in place, so GitHub's release email includes them.
