### Changed

- **Moving a tab feels like picking it up and putting it down.** Press on a row and move: it lifts off the list and follows your pointer, a little larger, with a deeper shadow. Before you let go, the other rows slide apart to make room exactly where it will land, and when you let go it settles in. A plain click still opens the tab. With the system's reduce-motion setting, the rows just move.
- **Esc puts it back.** So does carrying it out of the popup sideways. A drag never opens a tab by accident, wherever it ends.
- **A saved window lights up** while the tab you're carrying will go into it.
- **The pinned line holds.** Drag an unpinned tab up among the pinned ones and its place stops just below them; let go and it lands there, with a note saying why.
- **The same in Settings › Categories**, in the popup, the side panel and the library.
- **With a finger too.** On a touchscreen, hold a row still for a moment and it lifts; a swipe still scrolls the list, and a tap still opens the tab.
- **Site icons sit on a plain white tile in light mode.** The pastel square behind each icon, a different color per site, clashed with the row's category color; a site with no icon keeps its tinted letter. Dark mode is unchanged.

### Fixed

- A longer note at the bottom of the popup (such as why a tab stopped at the pinned line) was cut off after one line. Notes now wrap onto a second line.

### Under the hood

- Drag and drop is now the app's own, built on pointer events (no new library), in place of the browser's built-in one, whose ghost image, cursor and scrolling the app couldn't shape. Where a tab may land is the same tested rules as before, and the list shows the very order the drop writes. Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ (Option on a Mac) are unchanged.
- A steady 60 frames a second while dragging the first of 200 tabs most of the way down, holding it at the edge while the list scrolls: in the popup and the side panel, 1 and 2 frames of about 560 took over 20 ms; in Settings › Categories (60 categories), none of 530.
- A feature release: "New in 4.2" shows once after updating, in all eight languages.
- Two approved-screenshot checks that failed now and then are steady: Chromium sometimes repainted only part of the screen, drawing an edge a pixel differently, so those two tests now have it repaint whole areas.
- No new permissions, and nothing new is stored.
